# Lesson 1: Subject, Verb, Object (SVO)

### What is SVO?

English sentences normally follow this structure:

**Subject + Verb + Object**

- **Subject (S):** Who or what performs the action?
- **Verb (V):** The action or state.
- **Object (O):** Who or what receives the action?

### Examples

| Subject | Verb | Object |
|---|---|---|
| I | drink | coffee |
| She | reads | a book |
| He | drives | a car |
| They | order | food |
| My brother | cooks | dinner |
| We | need | a new table |

### Important Rules

**1. Word Order**

English normally follows SVO order.

- Incorrect: I coffee drink.
- Correct: I drink coffee.

**2. Subject is Normally Required**

English sentences normally need a clear subject.

- Incorrect: Is cold today.
- Correct: It is cold today.

**3. Dummy Subject (It)**

Use **it** when there is no specific subject, especially when talking about weather, time or temperature.

Examples:
- It is raining.
- It is cold today.
- It is 8 o'clock.
- It is getting dark.

**4. Not Every Sentence Needs an Object**

Some verbs can form complete sentences without an object.

Examples:
- I sleep.
- She works.
- He runs.

**5. How to Identify SVO**

Example: **Maria drives a red car.**

- Who drives? → Maria (Subject)
- What does Maria do? → Drives (Verb)
- What does Maria drive? → A red car (Object)

**6. Additional Sentence Elements**

A sentence can also include information about time, place or manner.

Example: **My brother cooks dinner every night.**

- Subject: My brother
- Verb: cooks
- Object: dinner
- Time: every night

### Key Point

**Subject = Who/What | Verb = Action/State | Object = Receiver of the action**

Not every English sentence has an object, but a normal complete statement needs a subject and a verb.
